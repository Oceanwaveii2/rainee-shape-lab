Recommended engineering path (project inference)
------------------------------------------------
Keep one editable canonical shape and local coordinates. Fit a selected rear
surface to each approved image, with camera, pose and shape variables separated.
Use the generated regional surfaces as soft shape cues after alignment, not as
four independent full-body replacements. Keep thigh shaping independent.

Use silhouette distance and selected surface distance losses rather than full
asset bounds or arbitrary tissue-volume estimates. Fix FBX stature and protected
landmarks. Solve nonnegative growth increments across accepted stages and retain
the final shape. Camera/pose may change after the endpoint without changing the
canonical rear state. Local mesh/SDF correction can repair continuity and poles;
a stable cage or deformation layer retains identity and editable controls.

Current native schedule is an initialization: closed-cage ratios 1, 1.5, 2,
2.5 at source 0, 10, 18, 26. The 27 native static frames and three hold samples
are checked for measured cage growth, constant stature, finite geometry and
sampled field orientation. This is not yet an image-fitted curve or walking,
contact, clothing or production-head solution.

Gaussian reference handling
---------------------------
SPZ is a compressed Gaussian representation. The official format distinguishes
centre position, anisotropic scale, rotation, opacity and appearance; coordinate
conventions and versions require explicit handling.
https://github.com/nianticlabs/spz

The local Spark 2.3.1 viewer displays actual anisotropic Gaussian data. It loads
one scene at a time and uses onDirty/on-demand rendering. A reversible box and
opacity threshold can isolate review regions and export centre statistics.
These are appearance samples, not an enclosed tissue solid or density estimate.
https://sparkjs.dev/docs/splat-mesh/
https://sparkjs.dev/docs/on-demand-rendering/

If a reviewed smooth deformation phi maps Gaussian means, a local covariance
update is mu'=phi(mu), Sigma'=J_phi Sigma J_phi^T. This is a first-order local
approximation for non-affine fields, not a guarantee for very large splats.
Only apply it to a segmented character region with an accepted canonical-space
transform; never deform the room as if its coordinates were Rainee's anatomy.

Surface alternatives researched
-------------------------------
2D Gaussian Splatting (SIGGRAPH 2024) represents surfaces using oriented planar
disks and introduces depth-distortion and normal-consistency constraints. It is
a useful surface-reconstruction direction when calibrated views exist; these
ordinary SPZ exports do not become 2DGS merely by being loaded.
https://surfsplatting.github.io/

Gaussian Opacity Fields (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024, journal track) extracts a level set
from a regularized Gaussian opacity field using scene-adaptive tetrahedra.
That provides a possible geometry route, not proof that a supplied unsegmented
appearance field yields reliable anatomy. Its reconstruction pipeline has not
been installed, trained or run here.
https://niujinshuchong.github.io/gaussian-opacity-fields/

Sketch-guided Cage-based 3D Gaussian Splatting Deformation (WACV 2026) uses
silhouette sketches, cage deformation and a variant of Neural Jacobian Fields.
Its published pipeline also uses diffusion/ControlNet priors. Only the cage
and Jacobian design ideas are relevant to our non-diffusion implementation;
the published system is not claimed as implemented or used.
https://tianhaoxie.github.io/project/gs_deform/

NeRF/appearance-only splats remain useful for look and view inspection, while
an editable connected surface/cage is the practical present authoring backbone.
Future fitting needs reviewed masks, pose/camera alignment and held-out views.
No training, diffusion generation, cloud upload or model installation occurred.
